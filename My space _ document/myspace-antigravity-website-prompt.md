# Antigravity Prompt — Build the My Space Website

Copy and paste the following prompt into Antigravity.

---

## Role

Act as a senior product designer, UX strategist, SEO specialist, brand designer, and full-stack web developer. Build a polished, responsive, production-ready website for **My Space Engineering, Construction & Valuers**, a construction and built-environment company based in Bangalore / Bengaluru, India.

The website must be designed as an **audience-first property decision experience**, not as a generic construction company brochure.

The website should help visitors immediately understand:

- What My Space does.
- Which service is relevant to them.
- How the process works.
- How My Space reduces uncertainty.
- What the visitor should prepare.
- How to start a conversation.

The core user experience should follow this sequence:

> **Concern → Clarity → Drawing → Construction → Finished Space → Conversation**

---

## 1. Business and Brand Context

**Business name:** My Space Engineering, Construction & Valuers  
**Brand promise:** Build Your Own Space  
**Location:** Bangalore / Bengaluru, India  
**Primary audience:** Property owners and decision-makers planning residential construction, commercial construction, civil work, interior design, elevation design, 3D floor plans, or property valuation.  
**Primary conversion:** Qualified enquiry.  
**Secondary conversions:** Phone call, WhatsApp conversation, service-page visit, project-page visit, process-page visit, and consultation preparation.

### Core services

1. Residential construction.
2. Civil and structural construction.
3. Commercial construction.
4. Interior design and execution support where applicable.
5. Elevation design.
6. 3D floor plan design.
7. Property valuation enquiries for bank, property, sale, purchase, tax, insurance, record, or other defined purposes, subject to verified professional scope.

### Brand personality

The website must feel:

- Elegant.
- Calm.
- Precise.
- Architectural.
- Trustworthy.
- Warm but professional.
- Technically credible.
- Smooth and easy to navigate.

It must not feel:

- Like a generic contractor catalogue.
- Like a real-estate listing portal.
- Like a loud sales website.
- Like a cheap template.
- Like a luxury fashion website disconnected from construction.
- Like a page filled with unsupported statistics or claims.

### Colour rules

- Use Midnight Navy for the header, footer, dark panels, and trust sections.
- Use My Space Blue for primary buttons, links, active states, and important interaction points.
- Use Sky Mist for soft backgrounds, valuation sections, forms, and supporting content.
- Keep Architectural Gold subtle and use it only as a premium accent.
- Let brick, wood, stone, concrete, glass, and green appear mainly through real or clearly labelled project imagery.
- Do not make orange or red the main brand colour.
- Maintain WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.

### Typography

Use:

- **DM Serif Display** for major H1s, emotional statements, editorial headings, and project titles.
- **Manrope** for navigation, body text, buttons, labels, forms, cards, and technical explanations.

Use sentence case for major headings. Use uppercase only for short eyebrow labels.

### Visual language

Use meaningful visuals related to:

- Contemporary Bangalore homes.
- Residential construction.
- Commercial spaces.
- Interior design.
- Architectural drawings.
- Floor plans.
- Elevation studies.
- 3D visualizations.
- Concrete, brick, stone, wood, steel, glass, tile, lighting, and greenery.
- Site progress and construction details.
- Property inspection and documentation context for valuation.

The hero should combine a contemporary built space with subtle architectural drawing or floor-plan linework. Use negative space for readable text. The background should feel like a transition from idea to built space.

Do not use generic hard-hat stock photography, unrelated skyscrapers, random luxury homes, fake project photos, or AI-generated imagery presented as completed My Space work.

If real project images are unavailable, use clearly labelled placeholders or concept imagery. Never label a concept as completed work.

## 4. Homepage Structure

Build the homepage in this exact strategic order.

### 4.1 Header

Desktop navigation:

- Services
- Projects
- How It Works
- About
- Insights
- Contact
- Start a Project

Include a visible phone or WhatsApp utility action where appropriate.

Mobile header:

- Call action.

### 4.2 Hero

Use a large immersive hero section, not a small image beside a large block of text.

Content:

**Eyebrow:**

> BUILD WITH MORE CLARITY

**H1:**

> Planning to build, design, or value a space in Bangalore?

**Paragraph:**

> My Space brings engineering, construction, interiors, visual design, and property valuation enquiries into one clear journey.

**Primary CTA:** `Tell Us What You Are Planning`

**Secondary CTA:** `See How It Works`

Add four small service chips:

- I want to build a home.
- I need design clarity.
- I need interiors.
- I need a valuation.

Each chip should link to the relevant page or set the relevant enquiry form mode.

### 4.3 Reassurance strip

Create four compact reassurance statements:

- **Start with clarity:** Understand what needs to happen before work begins.
- **See the idea first:** Use drawings, elevations, and 3D plans to make decisions.
- **Know the next stage:** Follow a visible process from planning to handover.
- **Talk to one team:** Reduce confusion between design, execution, and coordination.

Do not use unsupported numbers, rankings, or guarantees.

### 4.4 Situation-led service selector

Eyebrow:

> WHAT ARE YOU PLANNING?

H2:

> Choose the next decision you need to make.

Create six image-led cards in a varied editorial layout:

1. **Build a home** — Starting with a plot, idea, or unfinished project?
2. **Shape a commercial space** — Need a practical space for your business or operations?
3. **Make the inside work** — Need better layout, storage, materials, and finish?
4. **See it before building** — Need an elevation, floor plan, or 3D visualization?
5. **Strengthen the build** — Need civil and structural execution support?
6. **Understand the property** — Need a property or bank valuation enquiry?

Each card must have a relevant image, short description, and service-specific link.

### 4.5 Pain-point section

H2:

> Most property stress begins before the first brick is placed.

Show four concerns:

- **Unclear scope** — Decisions keep changing because no one defined the work clearly.
- **Disconnected teams** — Design, construction, vendors, and execution do not communicate.
- **Difficult visual decisions** — The client cannot understand the result before it is built.
- **Uncertain documentation** — Valuation or property decisions stall because requirements are unclear.

Use a constructive tone. Immediately follow this section with the solution process.

### 4.6 Solution process

H2:

> A clearer way forward, from first conversation to final outcome.

Display a five-stage process:

1. **Understand** — Goals, site, property, purpose, and constraints.
2. **Plan** — Scope, assumptions, decisions, and direction.
3. **Visualize** — Drawings, elevations, 3D plans, materials, and references.
4. **Build** — Execution, coordination, updates, and quality checks.
5. **Handover** — Review, documentation, completion items, or report pathway.

Desktop: horizontal timeline.  
Mobile: vertical timeline.

### 4.7 Drawing-to-built journey

H2:

> See the thinking before you see the finished space.

Show this visual sequence:

```text
Brief → Sketch → Floor Plan → Elevation → Construction → Finished Space
```

Use architectural drawings, dimensions, materials, construction progress, and finished-space visuals. A smooth scrollable editorial sequence is preferred over a heavy interactive slider.

### 4.8 Projects

Eyebrow:

> SELECTED WORK

H2:

> Spaces shaped around how people live and work.

Each project card must include:

- Project type.
- General location if approved.
- Client need or brief.
- My Space contribution.
- Status: Completed, Ongoing, or Concept.
- Image or drawing.
- View Project link.

Do not publish fake projects, invented testimonials, or unapproved client images.

### 4.9 Materials and details

H2:

> Details that shape the experience.

Use a tactile material strip featuring:

- Concrete.
- Brick.
- Stone.
- Wood.
- Metal.
- Glass.
- Tile.
- Lighting.
- Greenery.

### 4.10 Elevation and 3D floor plans

H2:

> See the space before it is built.

Create two large visual panels:

- **Elevation Design** — Façade drawing transitioning into a rendered exterior.
- **3D Floor Plans** — Technical plan transitioning into a furnished spatial view.

Include this limitation:

> Visualizations communicate design intent. Final construction and approval drawings depend on project scope and qualified technical review.

### 4.11 Property valuation route

H2:

> Need a valuation for a bank, property decision, or record?

Show:

> Enquire → Share documents → Inspect → Assess → Report

Explain:

- Purpose of valuation.
- Documents that may be required.
- Inspection expectations.
- Assessment and report scope.
- Professional limitations.

Mandatory disclaimer:

> A valuation does not guarantee loan approval. The final scope depends on the property, documents, inspection, and applicable professional requirements.

Do not claim “bank approved property valuer,” “registered valuer,” or similar credentials unless verified and supplied by the business owner.

### 4.12 Trust section

H2:

> Thoughtful planning. Clear execution.

Show four principles:

- Clear scope.
- Visible decisions.
- Coordinated communication.
- Accurate expectations.

Use real evidence, approved credentials, process details, or factual explanations instead of unsupported claims.

### 4.13 FAQ section

Create an accessible accordion with these questions:

1. I am not sure where to start. Can I still contact My Space?
2. Do you handle both residential and commercial construction?
3. Can I request only a floor plan, elevation, or 3D visualization?
4. Do you provide interior design with execution support?
5. How are construction scope and estimates explained?
6. What information is useful for a first consultation?
7. How does a property or bank valuation enquiry work?
8. Does a valuation guarantee bank approval?
9. Which areas of Bengaluru do you serve?
10. Can I view examples of completed or ongoing work?

### 4.14 Final CTA

H2:

> You do not need to have everything figured out.

Paragraph:

> Tell us what you are planning, what is unclear, or where you need help. We will start with the right questions.

Form fields:

- Name.
- Phone number.
- Email, optional.
- What do you need help with?
- Project location.
- Message.

Service options:

- Build a home.
- Commercial construction.
- Civil construction.
- Interior design.
- Elevation or 3D floor plan.
- Property valuation.
- Not sure yet.

CTA:

> **Start the Conversation**

Success message:

> Thank you. We have received your enquiry. You do not need to have every detail ready; our team will review your requirement and contact you through your preferred method.

## 7. Content and Copywriting Rules

Write as a calm, technically credible guide. Do not write like an aggressive salesperson.

### Preferred style

- Clear.
- Plain-language.
- Specific.
- Helpful.
- Evidence-led.
- Transparent about limitations.
- Short paragraphs.
- Descriptive headings.
- Strong but honest CTAs.

### Avoid

- Best in Bangalore.
- No. 1.
- Guaranteed results.
- Lowest price guaranteed.
- Loan approval guarantee.
- World-class solutions.
- Dream home clichés.
- Fake statistics.
- Unsupported years of experience.
- Unverified awards or certifications.
- Unapproved testimonials.
- Unapproved client logos.

### Cost copy rule

If showing construction or interior cost information, explain:

- Built-up area.
- Site access.
- Structural complexity.
- Material and finish quality.
- Services and approvals.
- Design scope.
- Labour and execution conditions.
- Project timeline.
- Date of information.
- Inclusions and exclusions.

Never present an educational estimate as a fixed quotation.

### Valuation copy rule

Clearly distinguish property valuation, valuation report, market opinion, construction estimate, title verification, and bank decision. Never imply that a valuation guarantees loan approval.

## 8. Technical Stack and Architecture

Build a fast, maintainable, content-managed, server-rendered website.

### Recommended stack

- Next.js with TypeScript.
- React.
- Tailwind CSS with design tokens.
- Radix UI or equivalent accessible primitives where useful.
- Headless CMS such as Sanity or Strapi.
- PostgreSQL only if structured operational data requires it.
- Server-side form endpoint.
- Zod or equivalent schema validation.
- Transactional email provider such as Resend, Postmark, or SendGrid.
- Google Analytics 4 or privacy-conscious equivalent.
- Google Search Console.
- Sentry or equivalent error monitoring.
- Vercel, Cloudflare Pages, or equivalent managed hosting.
- GitHub with pull-request workflow.

### Rendering rules

Use static generation or server rendering for public SEO content. Use client components only for interactions such as:

- Mobile navigation.
- FAQ accordions.
- Progressive enquiry form.
- Project filters.
- Galleries.
- Motion interactions.

Do not render the entire homepage only on the client. Core copy must be available to search crawlers in the initial rendered HTML.

### Suggested component structure

Create reusable components for:

- Header.
- Navigation.
- Mobile menu.
- Hero.
- Buttons.
- Service cards.
- Project cards.
- Process timeline.
- Material strip.
- FAQ accordion.
- Enquiry form.
- Valuation process module.
- Breadcrumbs.
- Footer.
- Mobile contact bar.

### CMS content types

Create editable CMS models for:

- Service.
- Project.
- Insight article.
- FAQ.
- Testimonial, only if approved.

- Navigation.
- SEO metadata.

The CMS must support draft, preview, publish, update, and rollback workflows.

## 11. Accessibility and Performance

Target WCAG 2.2 AA.

Implement:

- Semantic HTML.
- Keyboard navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- Correct heading hierarchy.
- Labelled forms.
- Accessible errors and success messages.
- Accessible FAQ accordions.
- Descriptive links.
- Useful image alt text.
- Empty alt text for decorative imagery.
- Sufficient colour contrast.
- Reduced-motion support.
- No essential content dependent on hover.

Performance requirements:

- Optimize and compress all images.
- Use AVIF or WebP where supported.
- Use responsive image sizes.
- Reserve image dimensions to avoid layout shift.
- Load below-the-fold images lazily.
- Avoid heavy third-party scripts.
- Keep the homepage mostly server-rendered.
- Optimize the hero image and fonts carefully.
- Test mobile performance on slow networks.

## 15. Acceptance Criteria

The website is complete when:

1. A visitor can understand the business and choose a service within the first screen.
2. The hero addresses the visitor’s uncertainty and provides a clear next action.
3. Construction, architecture, interiors, drawings, materials, and valuation are represented visually and intentionally.
4. Every service has a dedicated, SEO-focused page.
5. The page content follows the audience concern → explanation → process → proof → CTA model.
6. The drawing-to-built journey is easy to understand.
7. Projects are labelled Completed, Ongoing, or Concept accurately.
8. The valuation path is separate, clear, and compliant.
9. The enquiry form begins with low friction and reveals relevant fields progressively.
10. Call, WhatsApp, and enquiry actions are visible on mobile.
11. All public content is crawlable and metadata is unique.
12. No unsupported statistics, guarantees, rankings, bank claims, credentials, or testimonials are published.
13. The design uses the approved blue-led palette and typography system.
14. The website is keyboard accessible and readable with reduced motion enabled.
15. The website is optimized for mobile performance.
16. Analytics identify which service path generated an enquiry.
17. The CMS allows approved content editors to update pages, projects, FAQs, insights, and media.
18. The production deployment includes security, monitoring, backups, and rollback documentation.

> **My Space helps you move from uncertainty to a clearer, more considered space.**

Start by creating the design system, content structure, and homepage. Then implement the reusable page templates and service pages. Use real or clearly labelled placeholder content until the business provides approved project assets, credentials, testimonials, and contact information.
